Description of procedure
General anesthesia was induced with a laryngeal mask airway, and intravenous cefazolin was given by the anesthesia team before incision. The patient was positioned supine with the right arm abducted on a hand table, and a well-padded pneumatic tourniquet was placed on the upper arm. The arm was prepped and draped, and a time-out confirmed the right side. The limb was exsanguinated with an Esmarch bandage and the tourniquet was inflated to 250 mm Hg. Loupe magnification (3.5x) was used throughout.

A 10 cm curvilinear incision was made just posterior to the medial epicondyle. Two branches of the medial antebrachial cutaneous nerve crossing the distal field were identified and protected with a vessel loop throughout. The ulnar nerve was found proximally behind the medial intermuscular septum. The arcade of Struthers was released 8 cm above the epicondyle, and 4 cm of the medial intermuscular septum was excised so that the nerve would not kink after transposition. Osborne's ligament was divided; beneath it the nerve was flattened and hyperemic, with a fusiform swelling just proximal to the tunnel. Distally, the fascia between the two heads of the flexor carpi ulnaris and the deep flexor-pronator aponeurosis were released for 5 cm into the forearm. The nerve was then mobilized circumferentially from the arcade of Struthers to the flexor carpi ulnaris, preserving its accompanying vessels where possible. The small articular branch to the elbow joint was sacrificed to permit transposition, and the motor branches to the flexor carpi ulnaris were preserved. Internal neurolysis was not required.

The nerve was transposed anterior to the medial epicondyle into a subcutaneous bed superficial to the flexor-pronator mass. A 1.5 by 2 cm flap of flexor-pronator fascia based on the medial epicondyle was elevated and sutured to the subcutaneous tissue to form a sling that keeps the nerve from returning posteriorly. Through a full range of elbow motion the nerve stayed anterior, without tension or kinking proximally at the septum or distally at the flexor carpi ulnaris.

The tourniquet was released after 52 minutes and hemostasis was obtained with bipolar cautery. The wound was irrigated and local anesthetic was infiltrated. The subcutaneous tissue was closed with 3-0 absorbable sutures and the skin with a 4-0 monofilament subcuticular suture. A sterile dressing and a well-padded posterior long-arm plaster splint, from the upper arm to the metacarpal heads with the elbow at 90 degrees, the forearm in neutral and the wrist in slight extension, were applied, leaving the fingers free.
