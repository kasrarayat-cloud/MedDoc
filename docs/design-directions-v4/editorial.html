The Editorial Direction · A Manifesto

This is EDITORIAL. We believe a patient's note is a story. We refuse the silent gravity of tech UI — the cold gradient, the smug glass blur, the apologetic lowercase. Headlines belong on top. Signed · 11:52 ICD I48.0 CPT 99214 Visit QM-V-7807

Eleanor R. Whitfield, 72, returns with a quickening rhythm.

Three days of palpitations and a single near-syncope at the post office. EKG confirms paroxysmal atrial fibrillation. The patient leaves with apixaban, a Holter, and a follow-up in two weeks.

By Dr. Nina Chen · Internal Medicine · 14 min read · Filed 11:47 · Signed 11:52 · April 29, 2026

The patient, a retired English professor, presented today after three episodes of palpitations and one near-syncopal event in line at the post office. She rates the sensation as "a sudden tide that comes and goes," lasting two to ten minutes, without chest pain or radiation.

On examination, blood pressure was 138/82, pulse irregularly irregular at 104, lungs clear, no jugular venous distension. A 12-lead EKG performed in the room demonstrated atrial fibrillation with rapid ventricular response, no acute ischemic changes. Bedside echocardiogram by point-of-care ultrasound was unremarkable for pericardial effusion.

Plan: Initiated apixaban 5 mg twice daily after CHA2DS2-VASc scoring of 4 (age, female, hypertension, diabetes). Started metoprolol succinate 25 mg daily for rate control. Holter monitor placed for 48 hours. Cardiology referral within seven days for rhythm-control discussion. Patient educated on bleeding precautions; agreed and signed the informed-consent addendum.

Follow-up in two weeks with INR-irrelevant DOAC labs (creatinine, complete blood count) and a repeat 12-lead. The patient was advised to seek emergency care for any chest pain, severe shortness of breath, slurred speech, or one-sided weakness.
