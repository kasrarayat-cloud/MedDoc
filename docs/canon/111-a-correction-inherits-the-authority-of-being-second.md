# A CORRECTION INHERITS THE AUTHORITY OF BEING SECOND WITHOUT EARNING IT

**Standing 2026-09-05, from two instances in one session — the second while
correcting the first.**

A retraction arrives with a credibility a first statement never gets. It sounds
like the product of scrutiny: somebody checked, found themselves wrong, and
said so. That impression is doing work the evidence usually has not done, and
**a wrong correction is more expensive than the wrong claim it replaces**,
because the audience has already updated once and will not update twice.

⚠️ **REFERENCE CASE, AND IT WENT TO THE OWNER TWICE.** I told him a rendering
claim: *"the document does not move."* Reading an adversarial lens's summary, I
retracted it — the lens had shown the status was inert where it mattered, and I
read that as refuting the byte-level claim. **The retraction was the error.**
Two lenses had independently RENDERED the document and measured
`word/document.xml` byte-identical in every combination. The original claim was
correct.

What made the retraction feel safe is the whole lesson: it was **self-critical**,
and self-criticism reads as rigour. Retracting your own claim in favour of a
subagent's report is a move that cannot look like carelessness, so nobody
challenges it — least of all the person making it.

⚠️ **AND THE FIRST INSTANCE, EARLIER THE SAME DAY, HAD THE SAME SHAPE.** A
build-context measurement was corrected once correctly and then a second time
INCORRECTLY, the second correction confusing one container's context for
another's. Same session, same pattern: the later statement won on recency
rather than on evidence.

**THE PRACTICE, AND IT IS ONE QUESTION:**

> **Before retracting, ask what MEASUREMENT refutes the original — and if the
> answer is "a report I read", the original stands until you run something.**

- A lens, a subagent, a log or a colleague reporting a *related* fact is not a
  refutation of a *different* claim. Check the two claims are actually about
  the same proposition; the retraction above conflated *the status is inert*
  with *the bytes differ*, which are compatible.
- State the provenance of the correction as loudly as the correction: *"I
  re-ran it and X"* versus *"I read a report suggesting X"*.
- **Corrections are not free.** Each one spends credibility on the channel, and
  a channel that self-corrects twice on one fact stops being usable for the
  thing it exists to carry.

**Related:** *an unfalsifiable observation is not evidence, whoever made it*;
*a documented-unreliable instrument is not a permanently-wrong one* — of which
this is the mirror aimed at yourself; and *hypothesize from reading, report
only after running*.
