# A REGISTERED CONCEPT NOBODY TELLS THE PROPOSER ABOUT IS **UNMAPPABLE**

**Ratified 2026-09-07.** The registry is a gate, not an advertisement, and the
two are different lists.

`isRegisteredConcept` decides whether a mapping to a concept is **ALLOWED**.
`TEMPLATE_TWIN_CANONICAL_CONCEPT_GUIDANCE` decides what the proposer is **TOLD
EXISTS** — it is spliced into the model's prompt as the vocabulary to choose
from.

> **A concept in the first and not the second is legal, unreachable, and silent.**
> Nothing refuses it. It simply never gets proposed, and the control it was
> registered for stays `manual` forever.

`total_time_minutes` shipped that way and the adversarial pass caught it: the
acceptance row it exists to satisfy would have failed with every gate green and
no error anywhere.

## ⚠️ THE ARM IS SCOPED, AND MEASURING FIRST IS WHY

The obvious arm — *every registry concept appears in the guidance* — **reds
`main` on arrival.** Measured before writing it: **11 of 17** registry concepts
are legitimately absent, because guidance deliberately lists SUB-concepts
(`medication.name`, `allergy.substance`) rather than families (`medication`,
`allergy`), and omits the measurement kinds entirely.

So the arm names the concepts **this change added** and asserts they are in
BOTH. Same discipline as canon 114: *measure the population before writing a
rule that judges it* — a class-closing arm that reds on existing state is not a
stricter arm, it is a broken one that will be weakened or deleted within a day.

## THE GENERAL SHAPE

**A capability has an ALLOW list and an OFFER list, and they are maintained by
hand in different files.** This programme keeps meeting it: readers that were
updated and writers that were not (`conceptScope` had four readers and no
writer); a door that offers what the writer refuses; a gate that admits what
nothing proposes. **When you add to one, grep for the other** — and if you
cannot name the other list, that is the finding.
