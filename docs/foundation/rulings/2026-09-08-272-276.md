## #272 — OWNER-RULING — **A RECORD-DERIVED REFUSAL NEVER WITHHOLDS A DOCUMENT**

**STANDING. This decides the owner-class (b) question raised by PR #1057.**

> On a **REQUIRED** control, *"the record cannot answer"* degrades to
> **blank-with-a-named-reason**, and the control is routed to **the doctor's own
> fill** — the *"Your blanks"* surface — **exactly as a manual field.**
> **Never to blocking → `render: null`.**

**THE TRADE, STATED AS THE OWNER STATED IT:** *a withheld consult over one
ambiguous age is the wrong trade; a doctor typing "61" is the right one.*

⚠️ **THIS IS THE HALF THE SESSION SURFACED AND DID NOT DECIDE, AND IT WAS RIGHT
NOT TO.** #264's refusal widened a pre-existing trigger — `origin/main` already
routed a required age control with a missing DOB into
`missingResolution → needs_clinician_input → blocking → render: null`. The
session established that, corrected the finding's severity in both directions,
and escalated the trade rather than choosing it. **The escalation was correct and
the answer is that the pre-existing behaviour is ALSO wrong for this class.**

**Related and consistent:** **#157(b)** already rules that *no denial may refuse
an export*, and `FIELD_DENIAL_WITHHELD` is a warning **unconditionally** for
exactly that reason. This extends the same logic one field over: a record that
cannot answer is not a reason to withhold the other hundred and forty-nine
fields.

**THE ARM:** an ambiguous age on a **required** control yields a **RENDERED**
document with that field **blank** and its **reason in the manifest**.

**PRACTICE-TZ retires the refusal later** (see the board filing). Until then this
is how it behaves.

## #273 — OWNER-RULING — **ONE GUARD** (this closes #265(b))

**STANDING.**

> Keep **`gate-ruling-row-identity`** — the better-controlled one. **Fold any arm
> unique to `gate-ledger-reachability`'s duplicate check into it. Delete the
> weaker copy.**

**WHY, IN ONE LINE:** *two guards for one property drift the day either is
fixed.*

⚠️ **AND THIS OVERRIDES THE ORDERING RULE #265(b) ORIGINALLY GAVE.** That ruling
said *"the first on `main` stays, the other lane deletes its copy on rebase"* —
which selects the **ledger** arm, because it landed 2026-08-27 against
row-identity's 2026-09-07. The session reported that **both had already merged**,
so there was no "other lane", and declined to delete either on the grounds that
literal compliance would remove the better-controlled implementation.

**That was the right escalation, and the answer is MERIT, not ORDER.** The
ordering rule exists to resolve a race between two lanes; when both have landed
there is no race left to resolve, and the question becomes which guard is worth
keeping. **The exported, separately-controlled one is.**

⚠️ **"FOLD ANY ARM UNIQUE TO THE OTHER" IS NOT OPTIONAL, AND IT IS THE PART THAT
CAN GO WRONG SILENTLY.** Deleting a describe block that carried one assertion
nothing else makes is a coverage loss that no test reports. The unique set is
**enumerated before anything is deleted**, and the enumeration is the evidence.

## #274 — OWNER-RULING — **BUILD THE INGEST ENTRY POINT** (#263's remaining act)

**STANDING. Its own PR, its own adversarial pass.**

> A **server-side FUNCTION**, not a route, callable from the in-container door
> (#262's `--in transcribe-md-worker`):
>
>     ingest from stored artifact BYTES  ->  new DRAFT compiled version
>
> **PRE-GUARDS, both, and it ABORTS otherwise:**
>
> 1. the artifact's **sha256 equals the pinned record**
> 2. the new version's **53 fieldIds are set-equal** to the **CERTIFIED**
>    version's

**WHY A FUNCTION AND NOT A ROUTE.** A route needs a session, a request and an
HTTP surface, and would make an irreversible act reachable by anything that can
reach the app. A function is callable from the ruled door and from nowhere else
that matters — and the ruled door already carries the declaration gate, the
single-container resolution and the image-revision check.

⚠️ **THE SECOND PRE-GUARD IS WHAT MAKES THE ACT SAFE, AND IT DEPENDS ON A
PROPERTY THAT MUST BE VERIFIED RATHER THAN ASSUMED.** `fieldId` is recorded as
being salted with the **whole-package sha256**, so re-ingesting the *same bytes*
should reproduce the *same fieldIds*. If that is true the set-equality guard is
exact. **If it is not true, the guard can never pass and the act is impossible** —
so the derivation is read from the code before the guard is written, not after.

## #276 — OWNER-RULING — **THE LENS SANDBOXES ARE DESIGN/ADMIN'S, AND #213's MARKER IS WHAT MAKES THEM REAPABLE**

**STANDING. Build the marker in this batch.**

Twenty `.lens-1018-*` / `.lens-r7-1018-*` worktrees sit in the shared tree. They
belong to **Design/Admin**, not to this lane, and this session was right not to
reap them — the close-out rule says the session that made scratch reaps it.

> **But "not mine" is not a disposal policy.** Left alone forever they are
> 12.8 GB of a shared deploy surface that no session feels entitled to touch, and
> the reason nobody can touch them is that **a sandbox carries no record of whose
> it is or when it was made.**

**#213's marker is that record**, and building it is what converts *"not mine"*
into *"abandoned, and safe to reap"*.

⚠️ **A LIVENESS SIGNAL IS PART OF THE MARKER, NOT AN OPTIONAL EXTRA.** A sandbox
in USE by a running lens must never be reaped, and this programme has a canon
entry about exactly how that measurement goes wrong: **`pgrep -f` returned a
false LIVE reading for three hours** because the probe matched itself. The marker
must be readable by a session that did not create it, and the liveness question
must be answerable without a probe that can match the asker.
