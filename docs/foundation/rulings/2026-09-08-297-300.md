## #297 — OWNER-RULING — RECLAIM, IN THIS ORDER, THROUGH THE WRAPPERS

**STANDING. CLASS (d) — it deletes things, and one of them is a rollback path.**

Preflight went 🔴 on disk at **91%**, and both sanctioned reclaims freed **0 B**:
`builder prune --until=72h` found nothing older than 72 h, and
`prune-rollback-tags.sh` reported **72 dated tags = 72 kept/held + 0 deletable**.
The order below is the answer, and it is an ORDER — cheapest and most reversible
first.

**(a) `docker builder prune -af`, with NO `--until`.** Today's cache is
**regenerable**; the cost is slower builds for a day and nothing else.
⚠️ **DECLARE IT IN THE LOG**, so no other lane reads the slowdown as a fault.

**(b) THE ROLLBACK KEEP POLICY IS AMENDED — and this clause IS the ruling.**

> **Keep every tag younger than 72 h, PLUS the tags of the last FIVE JUDGED
> deploys. Everything older is a redeploy of an older commit anyway, not a
> rollback.**

`prune-rollback-tags.sh` implements the new policy, and:

- its **dry run lists what goes AND WHY** before the act;
- the act carries a **§2.5-style declaration**;
- ⚠️ **#137 STAYS, unchanged, for everything inside the keep-set.** The keep-set
  moved; the prohibition did not. `docker image prune -a` remains forbidden in
  every form.

**(c) RE-RUN PREFLIGHT. Proceed only under 90%.** Not "if it looks better" — the
gate is re-run and its verdict governs.

## #298 — OWNER-RULING — #255 STANDS; SEQUENCE, DO NOT SHARE

**STANDING.**

> **One sandbox per agent, verifiers included.** If RAM says the round cannot run
> in parallel — and **swap is exhausted** — the round runs **SEQUENTIALLY BY
> LANE**. It does **not** run by sharing sandboxes.

⚠️ The distinction is the whole clause. Sequencing costs wall-clock; sharing a
sandbox costs the property #255 exists to buy, and a lens that can see another
lens's tree is not an independent lens.

## #299 — OWNER-RULING — THE PREFLIGHT SKILL DESCRIBES THE BOX IT RUNS ON

**STANDING.**

Its Check 3 preamble states **13 GB RAM / 8 cores**. The box is **32 GB / 14
cores**.

> **A threshold calibrated against a machine that no longer exists is the
> WATCHED-SET failure in prose.**

The instrument is fine and its numbers still function — they are absolute, and
the real box is larger, so they are conservative. What is wrong is the stated
baseline a future reader would calibrate a NEW threshold from. Fix the preamble
to the box.
