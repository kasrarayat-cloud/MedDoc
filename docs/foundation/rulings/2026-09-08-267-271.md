## #267 — OWNER-RULING — THE CHART OWNS THE NOTE

**STANDING. This closes `SF-1` and generalises past it.**

Round eight found the insert door stamping its override row with the visit
**AUTHOR's** practice while the sign-time freeze reads the table with the
**TENANT's** — so a locum inserts a default, watches it appear in the preview,
and signs a document without it.

> **T3's rule stands: a visit's tenant derives from the PATIENT, never from the
> author. Every row attached to a visit carries `visit.practiceId`, READ FROM
> THE VISIT ROW ITSELF — the override write, the freeze, the fill cache, and
> the lock's predicate.**

**AUTHORIZATION IS A SEPARATE QUESTION AND NEVER A SOURCE FOR A STORED
`practiceId`.** Whether an actor may touch THIS visit — as its author, or as a
member of the tenant practice — is answered at the door. What gets written into
a row is answered by the visit. Conflating the two is what produced a row the
writer could see and the signer could not.

⚠️ **ONE DERIVATION, ONE SOURCE.** The failure was not a wrong value; it was
**three sources for one fact** — `session.user.practiceId`, the resolver's
returned `visit.user.practiceId`, and the visit row's own column — with
different subsystems reaching for different ones. Two of them agreeing on
current data is what kept it invisible: the door's own comment already records
*"58 of 58 visits have `transcribe_visits.practiceId` equal to their author's …
a property of the data, not of the code."*

**THE ARM CHANGES WITH IT.** *"The visit's own doctor, not the requester"*
becomes ***"not the author either — the VISIT's"***, and the fixture must give
all three values distinct, or it cannot witness the confusion it is named for.

## #268 — OWNER-RULING — THE DEADLOCK IS A WRITER-ORDER DEFECT, AND IT IS FIXED AT THE WRITERS

**STANDING.**

Round five reproduced **10 of 10 deadlocks at the DEFAULT isolation level** on
two legal concurrent owner demotions — confirmed independently by the
orchestrator before being believed.

**THE DIAGNOSIS, and it is why no trigger-side fix can work:** the `UPDATE`
locks its user row **before the trigger runs**, so the trigger's practice lock
is taken SECOND. Two demotions of different users therefore acquire their row
locks in opposite orders and deadlock — a lock ordering the trigger cannot
repair, because by the time it executes the first lock is already held.

> **ONE HELPER — `withPracticeLock` — takes the practice advisory lock BEFORE
> any user-row write, and EVERY user-row writer goes through it. The trigger
> KEEPS its own lock as defence: same key, same session, re-entrant.**

⚠️ **THE TRIGGER'S LOCK IS NOT REMOVED, AND THAT IS DELIBERATE.** A helper is
application code and can be bypassed by the next writer somebody adds; the
trigger cannot. Belt and braces, and the two must use the **same key in the same
session** or the second acquisition is a second lock rather than a re-entry.

**THE PROOF IS A NUMBER, STATED IN ADVANCE:** 10/10 deadlocks → **0/10**, with
both demotions landing or one refused **by name**, and `40P01` never observed.

## #269 — OWNER-RULING — THE GUARD REFUSES ABOVE READ COMMITTED

**STANDING. It replaces a claim with a measurement.**

`migration.sql:234` asserts *"all three isolation levels are now safe, and the
two that are not READ COMMITTED fail LOUDLY rather than silently."* Five round-
five findings refute it, and **`MIG-3` established that no proof anywhere runs a
transaction above READ COMMITTED** — so the sentence was never measured at all.

**THE GUARD CANNOT BE CORRECT ABOVE READ COMMITTED, AND THE REASON IS
STRUCTURAL:** a snapshot taken before the lock is acquired **never refreshes**,
so the transaction waits its turn correctly and then counts owners on a view of
the world from before the other writer committed. No amount of locking repairs a
stale snapshot.

> **So it REFUSES there — `RAISE` naming the isolation level — and the proof runs
> at ALL THREE levels, asserting the refusal at the two elevated ones.**

**The comment is replaced by what is measured.** ⚠️ *A comment that asserts a
property no proof exercises is worse than silence: it answers the question a
reader would otherwise go and check.*

**AND THE OTHER HALF IS AN AUDIT, NOT AN ASSUMPTION.** User-role writers run at
READ COMMITTED **by construction — the helper asserts it**. The five shipped
`Serializable` sites are audited for user-row writes, and any found **is moved
out**. ⚠️ Refusing inside a `Serializable` transaction that legitimately needs to
write a user row would convert this guard into an outage, so the audit precedes
the refusal rather than following it.

## #270 — OWNER-RULING — #258's MECHANISM DEVIATION IS ACCEPTED, AND #258 IS AMENDED

**STANDING — AMENDS #258.**

#258 named its mechanism: *"`scaffoldDefaultOfferable` CALLS the resolver's own
`missingResolution` predicate — the real function, exported."* The branch did the
opposite — the **resolver walks the OFFER's table** — on a measured bundle
argument, and `scaffoldDefaultOfferable` reads the resolution's own `reasonCode`.

> **AMENDED TO: *one implementation, EITHER DIRECTION*. The resolver walking the
> offer's table satisfies the invariant.**

⚠️ **THE INVARIANT WAS NEVER THE CALL DIRECTION.** #258's argument is that *a
second implementation of a rule is a defect testing can only FIND, never
prevent* — which is discharged by there being ONE copy, whichever module holds
it. This is #200's precedent applied again: **a ruling binds its INVARIANT and
its PROOF, never its MECHANISM**, and the session that meets the invariant by a
better route has complied rather than deviated.

**IT WAS RIGHT TO SURFACE IT RATHER THAN ABSORB IT.** The deviation was measured
and reported instead of being quietly reconciled with the ruling's wording, and
that is the behaviour being ratified here — not merely the outcome.
