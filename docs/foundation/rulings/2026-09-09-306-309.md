## #306 — OWNER-RULING — THE NARROWING STANDS, AND WINDING DOWN GETS ITS OWN DOOR

**STANDING.**

> **An unrecoverable practice is never a legitimate outcome of two ordinary
> clicks.**

The `users_last_owner_guard` narrowing lands as built: exemption 2 counts
RESTORABLE members rather than LIVE ones, refusing only the corner where a
trapped non-owner remains and no restorable owner can rescue the practice.
Suspending a solo OWNER stays legal, because that owner is their own rescuer.

⚠️ **AND THE CAPABILITY IT NARROWS IS NOT DISMISSED — IT IS RE-HOMED.** Winding
a practice down is a real need, and the answer is not to leave it reachable as a
side effect of suspend-then-delete. It becomes **PRACTICE-ARCHIVE**, an ADMIN
lifecycle door: **ONE DECLARED ACT** that archives the practice AND its members
together, restorable, audited — the same row `PRACTICE_ARCHIVED` was deferred
to.

The distinction the ruling turns on: today's path reaches the end state by
**accretion**, one legal click at a time, with no actor ever deciding "this
practice is closing". PRACTICE-ARCHIVE makes that a decision somebody takes,
names, and can undo.

## #307 — OWNER-RULING — THE SECOND APP IS RETIRED, SO ITS DOORS ARE MADE UNREACHABLE

**STANDING.**

`meddoc-pro` is retired: its container has been down five weeks, `pro.quillmd.com`
serves **410 by design**, and it is already out of scope by standing rule. It
nevertheless remains a defined compose service, one `docker compose up` from
reviving **four unlocked `users` writers** whose missing lock was MEASURED to
deadlock the locked doors — the locked side losing, its write silently gone.

> **Remove the compose service definition and the image reference, so no
> `compose up` can revive an unlocked `users` writer.**

A ruled, **declared, git-reversible** config change, carrying the **co-tenant
baseline row** (`pro.quillmd.com` must still answer 410 — ⚠️ *a change AWAY from
410 is the finding*).

**The four doors stay PINNED until the code is gone.** Removing the service
closes the door; it does not delete the writer. **DELETE-MEDDOC-PRO** is filed
now as the arc item that removes the code, and the pin retires with it.

⚠️ **CUSTOM-FILE OWNS COMPOSE AND DEPLOY.** This is a **board filing to them**,
not an instruction from this lane — authorization travels owner → session and
never sideways through a peer.

## #308 — OWNER-RULING — THE SHARED TREE MUST TYPECHECK

**STANDING.**

**No dev server runs on the deploy surface.** A resident `next-server` on
`/opt/meddoc-pro` leaves stale generated `.next/types` behind, and the pre-push
hook's typecheck then **refuses every push from the shared tree — including a
branch deletion — naming a file the pusher never touched.**

Three parts:

1. **Identify the resident `next-server`'s owner session and stop it THERE.**
   Not killed from outside: a process on a shared box belongs to whoever started
   it.
2. **Clear the stale gitignored `.next` in the shared tree.**
3. **The hook's typecheck EXCLUDES `.next/types`**, so a generated artifact can
   never refuse a push again.

⚠️ **THE THIRD PART IS THE ONE THAT ENDS THE CLASS.** The first two fix today's
instance; only the exclusion stops the next regenerated artifact doing it again.

⚠️ **CUSTOM-FILE'S — THE HOOK IS THEIRS.** Board filing.

## #309 — OWNER-RULING — MERGE, AND THE UNEXPLAINED STAYS UNEXPLAINED

**RATIFIED.**

**Merge #1018 and #1036 through the wrapper on their clean citations.** They
ride the **NEXT composed deploy** with their rows; the re-issue deploy already in
flight does not wait for them.

⚠️ **AND THE `next@16.2.11` + #302 e2e INTERACTION STAYS RECORDED AS
UNEXPLAINED ON #1018.** Four failures with #302 on the old Next, a pass on
16.3.4 with #302 unchanged, two #302-free controls passing on the old version,
and one file between the failing and passing commits — `package-lock.json`. A
green that arrives without a mechanism is a green, not an explanation, and
writing it down as "fixed" would cost the next reader the two facts worth
starting from.
