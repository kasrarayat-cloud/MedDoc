## WHAT THIS CHANGE DOES

`deriveVerificationEncounter` returned a bilateral lower-extremity **venous
reflux** dictation for any form it could not address, so an operative note, a
SOAP template or a thyroid study certified against a leg-vein study. It now
REFUSES with a named reason, and both certification callers refuse through one
shared path below the mappings-readiness gates.

⚠️ **THIS IS AN OVER-PERMISSION FIX, SO THIS DECLARATION CARRIES ABSENCE ROWS.**
A presence-only declaration ("is the new code shipped?") passes just as happily
on the broken version. Row 7 is the one that can detect the old behaviour
surviving.

## WHAT THIS DEPLOY CANNOT PROVE, STATED PLAINLY

- **Zero production rows reach the new refusal.** 9 of 10 templates have no
  compiled twin at all, and the 1 that does is the certified venous form. The
  refusal path is unreachable in production today; this ships an invariant, not
  a repair to a live symptom.
- **No clinical walk backs it.** A real doctor's visit is never a smoke target,
  and the QA stack could not stand in either — its app/worker images are dated
  **2026-08-18** and predate F5-d/e/f/h. Named, not worked around.
- **The per-visit fill path is untouched** — the derivation has no caller on it
  (census: two certification callers, one prod-smoke harness, tests). So a
  certified twin's existing fills cannot be affected by this change.

## ROLLBACK

Tag `pre-<sha>-20260821` cut against the RUNNING image ids and digest-verified
before recreate. A rollback is a clean inverse here: the change adds a refusal
and removes a fallback, and reverting restores the fallback exactly. **No
capability is stranded by a rollback** — the cost of rolling back is the return
of the false-certificate behaviour, which is the defect, not a feature.
