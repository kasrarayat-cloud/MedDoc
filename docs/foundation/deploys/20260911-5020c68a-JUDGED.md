## ⚠️ THE FINDING — THE DISK WATCHDOG DISARMED ITSELF 12 SECONDS IN, AND THE DECLARATION IS WHAT TRIPPED IT

The declared watchdog (floor 6G, "armed until the first production recreate")
matched its disarm marker as a plain substring, `grep -F "=== 5b."`.
`deploy-prod.sh` prints the resolved declaration into the log at step 1, and
**this declaration's own watchdog paragraph quoted that marker**: "production
recreate (`=== 5b.`)", at log line 259. So the watchdog read the marker in the
declaration and stood down at **07:21:44Z**, while the real step-5b header did
not appear until line 1067 at 07:29:56Z.

That is the trap this lane filed as custom-file board #13, with a new vehicle:
**a marker the process prints BEFORE the moment it means.** Last time the
marker was a script header; this time it was the declaration describing the
guard.

**Found at 07:27:08Z** from the watchdog's own disk samples, which read
`armed=0` while the log's latest header was step 5. **Recovered at 07:28:37Z**
with a replacement guard. It found the deploy's process group without matching
its own command line, anchored the marker at line start (`^=== 5b\.`), and
re-checked the marker before any kill. It disarmed on the REAL header at
07:29:56Z and never fired.

**The exposure was real and small.** The build ran unguarded from 07:21:44Z to
07:28:37Z. The lowest free disk of the whole act was **12,740M**, at 07:29:57Z,
against a 6G floor. So the floor was never approached, and that was luck, not
the guard working.

**The durable fix is `deploy-watchdog2.sh`, which matches `^=== 5b\.` only.**
It was proven before its first use against both shapes, each under an
impossible floor so that any armed moment would kill:
- the marker quoted mid-line **stays armed and kills** (exit 97, the fake
  deploy never reached its end);
- the real header at line start **disarms and does not kill** (exit 0, the
  fake deploy reached its end).
