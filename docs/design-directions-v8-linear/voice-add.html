SUBJECTIVE

42-year-old female presents with a three-day history of productive cough, nasal congestion, and low-grade fever (Tmax 100.4 F at home). Reports mild fatigue and reduced appetite. Has been taking acetaminophen 500 mg every six hours with partial relief. Denies recent travel, sick contacts other than school-aged child with similar symptoms, or known TB exposure. Influenza vaccine current. No history of asthma or COPD.

OBJECTIVE

Vitals: T 99.6 F, HR 88, BP 122/76, RR 18, SpO2 97% on room air. Weight 62.4 kg. Patient appears mildly fatigued but in no acute distress.

HEENT: nasal mucosa erythematous with clear-yellow discharge; oropharynx mildly injected without exudate; tympanic membranes clear bilaterally. Neck supple, no cervical lymphadenopathy. Lungs: scattered coarse rhonchi in bilateral lower fields, clearing partially with cough; no wheezing, no rales, good air movement throughout. Cardiac: regular rate and rhythm, no murmurs.

ASSESSMENT

Acute bronchitis J20.9, most likely viral in etiology given gradual onset, family contact with similar illness, and absence of focal consolidation on exam. Patient is otherwise healthy without comorbid pulmonary disease, and presentation is consistent with self-limited course.

No clinical evidence of bacterial pneumonia: oxygenation preserved, no fever above 101 F today, no rigors, and lung exam shows clearing rhonchi rather than fixed crackles. Influenza unlikely given timing and vaccine status, but rapid antigen testing available if symptoms progress.

PLAN

Symptomatic management: continue acetaminophen 500-1000 mg PO every six hours PRN fever or discomfort, not to exceed 3 g daily. Add guaifenesin 600 mg ER twice daily for cough loosening. Encourage hydration to at least two liters daily and humidifier use overnight.

Patient education materials provided on viral bronchitis course (typically resolves within 10-14 days). Return precautions discussed: dyspnea at rest, fever above 101.5 F persisting beyond 72 hours, hemoptysis, or chest pain warrant prompt re-evaluation. Patient verbalized understanding.
