Assessment
Edit

1. Acute bronchitis (J20.9), resolving. Day 10 of illness with marked symptomatic improvement — residual mild productive cough is expected and consistent with the natural post-viral course. Lungs clear on exam, oxygen saturation normal, no constitutional symptoms. No antibiotics indicated; presentation remains consistent with viral etiology and clinical trajectory supports observation.

2. Bronchospasm, intermittent — improving. Albuterol use has tapered appropriately. Continue PRN through full resolution of cough.

Patient is medically appropriate for return to moderate activity (yoga, brisk walking) as tolerated; advised to use albuterol 15 minutes pre-exercise if symptomatic.

Plan
Edit

1. Continue albuterol HFA 90 mcg, 2 puffs PRN q4h for residual cough or wheeze. Counsel proper inhaler technique on this visit (first dispensed inhaler).

2. Supportive care: hydration, honey-based cough suppression at bedtime, humidifier use overnight. Tylenol or ibuprofen PRN if recurrent low-grade fever.

3. Expect full resolution within 7–10 additional days. Reassure patient that lingering mild cough up to 3 weeks post-onset is within normal range.

4. Return precautions: Worsening dyspnea, fever > 100.4°F > 48 hours, hemoptysis, chest pain, or O₂-saturation symptoms (lightheadedness with mild exertion) → return to clinic or urgent care same day.

5. Follow-up: PRN. Routine annual physical already scheduled for September; no separate bronchitis recheck required if course continues to improve.
